I§§8, 11, 31Proved consequences

The past passes no final sentence

Every finite past has two continuations: in one grasping and pramada end, in the other binding repetition continues. A finite observation therefore certifies neither final thawing nor final condemnation.

Scope of the claim

History permits every continuation. This is a modelling assumption, not proof that every future is available to a real jiva. IceDynamics supplies a unit update contract; repeated accretion and clearing are proved consequences. Neither habit psychology nor passage between incarnations is proved by them. Siddha is a compatibility alias for EventuallySiddha: eventually, not already now.

II§§17, 32Observation windows

Recognition is not a certificate

The three marks are read over nonzero windows and sequences of windows. Recognition can be withdrawn. The Emperor’s role includes support for another, but does not certify a siddha; final thawing does not assign that role either.

III§§30, 32, 35Contract and countermodels

Support does not become a throne

Supporting another, deciding for them, and expressing the whole are three different relations. One may support many without claiming their Riverbeds. No single voice expresses every aspect of the whole.

IV§§13, 29, 33Sufficiency and cost

Releasing the hand does not erase the wound

Protection is compared with available actions: stop harm without unnecessary restraint. Once the threat is gone, restraint is released and no new wound is inflicted. Damage already caused remains in the account.

Scope of the claim

Problem supplies adequacy and consequences. The theorem is conditional on those data. This is neither a complete definition of harm nor a claim that real consequences are known in advance.

V§§2, 8, 10, 24Structural contracts

Remove the wall, preserve the edge

The encounter has three structural threads. Removing a wall does not remove one’s edge. An acknowledged beast remains the same beast, but no longer belongs to the shadow; the shadow is not exhausted by beasts.

Scope of the claim

This defines a representation of concepts. Identity preserved by construction is not presented as a discovery about the psyche. Connection marks the presence of a link; neither the Source nor Hridaya’s voice is encoded as data.

VI§38Shared effects and the invariant

Each chooses; the circle responds together

The choice belongs to each jiva, but acts on the common circle. One jiva’s effective Shakti can open an exit for everyone still participating; another’s kashaya can imprison them all, even those intending to return. Each retains their own choice; everyone inside shares one outcome. An open passage is not a completed return: departure takes a personal step. Local recovery requires another companion with an open passage; a new explosion opens it for everyone.

Scope of the claim

CollectiveRule separately specifies which contribution is effective. The kernel assigns neither universal priority nor a numerical measure to Shakti and kashaya. The demonstration instance permits both outcomes when contributions conflict; it illustrates possibilities, not a law settling that conflict. ReturningPower requires the catalyst’s own reclaim. Excluding enclosure from Admissible is a normative choice, not a proved impossibility. The preservation theorem checks the other admissible transitions; recovery-reopens is conditional on a support witness.

VIIDoctrineContracts ≠ consequences

Each statement has its own status

Doctrine has two parts. ModelContracts states what the representation supplies. Theorems contains consequences proved under those definitions. The imperium instance supplies both parts.

Scope of the claim

Neither type checking nor the Doctrine record proves the truth of the entire teaching. Failure to construct a new instance is not itself proof of inconsistency: lemmas or premises may be missing.
